First aid · OHS Regulation Part 3 and Schedule 3-A

Level 1, 2 and 3 are gone. Your old tickets run out by November 2027.

November 26, 2026  ·  Derek Henderson  ·  5 min read

On November 1, 2024, WorkSafeBC rewrote the first aid rules for the first time in about 20 years. The certificate names changed, the training hours changed, and the table that sets how many attendants you need was replaced. Many employers haven't had to act yet, because old certificates still count. That grace runs out.

What changed

The certificate levels were aligned with the CSA first aid standards and renamed:

OldCourseNewCourse
Level 17 hoursBasic7 hours
Level 235 hoursIntermediate14 hours
Level 370 hoursAdvanced70 hours

The transportation endorsement didn't change: WorkSafeBC says it stays the same in name and training time. The big practical change is the middle level. Intermediate is a 14-hour course where Level 2 was 35, so replacing a Level 2 attendant costs a lot less time off the job than it used to.

Your old certificates, and the 2027 date

Schedule 3-A section 2 says a Level 1, 2 or 3 certificate issued before November 1, 2024 that hasn't expired counts as a Basic, Intermediate or Advanced certificate respectively. The same section says it is repealed on November 1, 2027.

In practice: every old ticket keeps working until its own expiry date, and after November 1, 2027 the old levels stop counting at all. If your attendant coverage depends on Level 1, 2 or 3 tickets, the renewal date on each card is your real deadline, and none of them gets you past November 2027.

What to check now

  • List your attendants and their expiry dates. Note which ones you rely on for each shift; Schedule 3-A requires the attendants on its list to be at the workplace during every shift where they're required.
  • Check the level you need under the new table. The requirements moved for many workplaces, with the biggest changes for higher-risk and remote work. The first aid requirements calculator looks it up from your workers, hazard rating and how remote the site is.
  • Book renewals at the new level. A Level 2 holder renews as Intermediate. If the new table says you need Advanced, renewal time is when to step up.
  • Check your kits. WorkSafeBC publishes contents lists for personal, basic, intermediate and advanced kits based on CSA Z1220. Older kits may not match.
  • Make sure the paperwork exists. The 2024 rules also require a written first aid assessment reviewed every 12 months (s.3.16), written first aid procedures (s.3.17) and a first aid drill at least once a year (s.3.17(4)).

From the field. The drill is the piece most small crews skip, and it's the one that finds the real problems: the radio channel nobody monitors, the gate that's locked when the ambulance arrives, the attendant who's on the other side of the site. Run it once and fix what it shows.

Common questions

Is my Level 2 certificate still valid?

Yes, until its expiry date. Schedule 3-A section 2 of the OHS Regulation treats a Level 1, 2 or 3 certificate issued before November 1, 2024 that has not expired as a Basic, Intermediate or Advanced certificate respectively. That section is repealed on November 1, 2027.

Is Intermediate first aid the same as Level 2?

It replaces it. WorkSafeBC lists the Intermediate course at 14 hours, down from 35 hours for Level 2. Basic stays at 7 hours like Level 1, and Advanced stays at 70 hours like Level 3.

Did the transportation endorsement change?

No. WorkSafeBC says the transportation endorsement remains unchanged in both name and training duration.

Do I need new first aid kits?

Check them. WorkSafeBC publishes contents lists for personal, basic, intermediate and advanced kits based on CSA standard Z1220, and the kit level your workplace needs comes from Schedule 3-A.
