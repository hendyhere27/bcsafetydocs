After a workplace incident in BC: immediate notice, Form 7, 48 hours, 30 days.

October 5, 2026  ·  Updated November 26, 2026  ·  Derek Henderson  ·  8 min read

Incident reporting in BC isn't in the OHS Regulation. It is in the Workers Compensation Act, Part 2, Division 10 (ss.68–73). The deadlines are short, and the first one is "immediately". Here is the sequence in the order you'll need it.

WhenWhatApplies to
ImmediatelyPhone WorkSafeBC (s.68)Serious injury or death, major structural failure, major hazardous release, dangerous fire or explosion
Within 3 daysForm 7, the Employer's Report of InjuryA worker missed time or needed medical attention beyond first aid
Within 48 hoursPreliminary investigation report (s.71)Every incident that must be investigated (s.69)
Within 30 daysFull investigation report to WorkSafeBC (s.72)Every incident that must be investigated (s.69)

Step 1: Immediate notice to WorkSafeBC (s.68)

An employer must immediately notify WorkSafeBC of any accident that:

  • resulted in serious injury to or the death of a worker,
  • involved a major structural failure or collapse of a building, bridge, tower, crane, hoist, temporary construction support system or excavation,
  • involved the major release of a hazardous substance,
  • involved a fire or explosion that had a potential for causing serious injury to a worker, or
  • was an incident required by regulation to be reported.

Section 68(2) also says that, unless an officer of the Board or a peace officer directs otherwise, nobody may disturb the scene of a reportable accident except as necessary to attend to injured or killed persons, prevent further injury or death, or protect endangered property.

The number to call is WorkSafeBC's Prevention line: 604.276.3301 in the Lower Mainland or 1.888.621.7233 toll-free.

Step 1b: Form 7, within 3 days

This is the report most employers actually file, and the one the immediate notice does not replace. WorkSafeBC says employers must report a workplace injury or disease within three days of the incident if the worker missed time from work or needed medical attention beyond first aid. The report is Form 7, the Employer's Report of Injury or Occupational Disease, filed online or by fax or mail.

  • Don't wait for every detail. WorkSafeBC says you can submit before you have the full picture.
  • File it even if you disagree. The form itself says the report is required even if you disagree with the claim.
  • Not reporting is an offence. WorkSafeBC says failing to report an injury can result in fines, and coercing a worker not to report is a separate offence.

Step 2: Which incidents must be investigated (s.69)

An employer must carry out a preliminary and a full investigation of any incident that is reportable under s.68, that resulted in injury requiring medical treatment, that involved no injury or only minor injury but had the potential to cause serious injury (a near miss with serious potential), or that was required by regulation to be investigated. Vehicle accidents on a public street or highway are excluded (s.69(2)).

Step 3: Who investigates (s.70)

The investigation must be carried out by persons knowledgeable about the type of work involved, with the participation of the employer or a representative and a worker representative if they are reasonably available. The employer must make every reasonable effort to have witnesses available for interview and must record their names, addresses and telephone numbers.

Step 4: Preliminary investigation, within 48 hours (s.71)

Immediately after the incident, the employer must start a preliminary investigation to identify unsafe conditions, acts or procedures that significantly contributed and the corrective action needed. The report must be completed within 48 hours of the incident, provided to WorkSafeBC on request, and provided to the joint committee or worker health and safety representative as soon as practicable, or posted at the workplace if there isn't one. Corrective action must follow without undue delay, with a report on the action taken.

Step 5: Full investigation, within 30 days (s.72)

Immediately after the preliminary investigation, the employer undertakes a full investigation to determine the cause or causes, identify unsafe conditions, acts or procedures, and determine corrective action. The full report must be submitted to WorkSafeBC within 30 days of the incident and, within that same 30 days, provided to the joint committee or worker health and safety representative (or posted). The Board may extend the period (s.72(3)).

You can't discourage a report. Section 73 prohibits an employer or supervisor from discouraging, impeding or dissuading a worker from reporting an injury, illness, death or hazardous condition to the Board, or from applying for or receiving compensation. It is also one of the circumstances that doubles an administrative penalty (see below).

What happens if you miss these

There is no fixed fine for a late report. WorkSafeBC has two main tools, and both scale with how serious the violation is and how big the employer is.

OHS citations (s.94)

A citation is the lower-level option, and it covers a narrower thing: failing to comply with a WorkSafeBC order or a required compliance report, not the original violation. A missed report usually leads to an order to produce it; ignore the order and a citation can follow. In 2026 the maximum citation is $1,312.96, adjusted each year for inflation. Under the OHS Citations Regulation, a first citation is half that, and a repeat within 3 years of a previous one is the full amount.

Administrative penalties (s.95)

WorkSafeBC's policy (Prevention Manual item P2-95-5) sets the amount in steps:

  • Basic amount: 0.5% of the employer's assessable payroll for the full calendar year before the violation, with a $1,250 minimum.
  • Doubled for each aggravating circumstance: a high-risk violation, an intentional violation, obstructing an officer (s.79), discouraging a worker from reporting (s.73), or breaching a stop-work or stop-use order. One circumstance makes it 2×, two make it 4×, three make it 6×.
  • Repeats: a prior penalty for the same or a similar violation within 3 years multiplies the amount by 2 for each prior penalty.
  • Ceiling: the statutory maximum is $816,148.69 for 2026, also adjusted every year.

Two more things matter. WorkSafeBC publishes the names of penalized employers, with the amount and a description, in a public database. And under s.95(3) a penalty must not be imposed if the employer shows due diligence: that it took all reasonable care to prevent the violation. Completed reports, written procedures and training records are what that defence is built on.

Common questions

Is Form 7 the same as the immediate notice?

No. The immediate notice under section 68 is a phone call to WorkSafeBC for serious incidents, made right away. Form 7, the Employer's Report of Injury or Occupational Disease, is the injury report. WorkSafeBC says the immediate notice does not replace Form 7, so a serious injury needs both.

When is Form 7 due?

WorkSafeBC says employers must report a workplace injury or disease within three days of the incident if the worker missed time from work or needed medical attention beyond first aid. You can submit it before you have every detail, and you still submit it if you disagree with the claim.

Do I have to investigate a near miss?

Yes, if it had the potential to cause serious injury. Section 69 requires a preliminary and a full investigation of incidents with no injury or only minor injury that had that potential, as well as injuries that needed medical treatment and anything reportable under section 68.

What is the penalty for missing a reporting deadline?

There is no fixed fine per late report. WorkSafeBC can order you to comply and impose an administrative penalty; ignoring an order can also bring an OHS citation (up to $1,312.96 in 2026). A penalty usually starts at 0.5% of the previous year's assessable payroll, with a $1,250 minimum, and it is doubled if the employer discouraged a worker from reporting (section 73). The 2026 maximum is $816,148.69.

Can I avoid a penalty if I tried to comply?

Section 95(3) says WorkSafeBC must not impose an administrative penalty if the employer shows it exercised due diligence: that it took all reasonable care to prevent the violation. Written procedures, training records and completed reports are the evidence that supports due diligence.
