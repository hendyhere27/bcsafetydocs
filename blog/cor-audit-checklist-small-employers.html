COR audit checklist for small employers: what to have ready.

November 27, 2026  ·  BC Safety Docs  ·  8 min read

A COR audit checks whether your health and safety program exists on paper and whether it is really running at work. This guide covers how an audit collects evidence, how it is scored, and what to have in hand for each of the 13 elements a small employer's written program is usually measured against. For the background on what COR is and who certifies you, start with our COR guide for small employers.

Read this first. Each certifying partner publishes its own audit tool, and the structure and wording differ. This checklist is a readiness view, not your partner's audit tool and not a pass or fail prediction. Confirm the details with your own partner.

Who audits a small employer

WorkSafeBC describes two kinds of auditor: an external auditor from outside your company, and an internal auditor, who is a permanent employee or dependent contractor trained by your certifying partner. WorkSafeBC says that if you are a small employer (fewer than 20 workers), an internal auditor may conduct both your certification audit and your annual maintenance audits. For employers with 20 or more workers, an internal auditor may conduct only the maintenance audits. The auditor has to be approved by your certifying partner, and your partner reviews the audit report afterwards. Partners set their own small-employer thresholds, so check yours.

How an audit gathers evidence

Expect more than a document review. go2HR's small-employer (SECOR) manual says each audit visit must include all three data collection methods: observation, documentation and interviews. The same manual says group interviews are not permitted, and it sets minimums for small employers: with fewer than 5 staff, every worker is interviewed, and with 18 to 19 staff the minimum is 12 interviews.

Those figures are go2HR's, from a manual dated March 2016 and reviewed June 2022. Other partners set their own numbers, but the practical point carries over: workers may be asked how things really work, so your records are only half of the preparation. The second half is workers who know where the policy is posted, how to do a field-level hazard check, and what to do in an emergency.

How the audit is scored

The partners we checked publish the same pass mark: 80% overall and at least 50% in every element. go2HR's manual adds that an audit below either line is not successful and is not registered with WorkSafeBC. One empty element can therefore fail an audit that is strong everywhere else. We checked the pass mark on BCCSA, AgSafe, go2HR, Energy Safety Canada and BC Forest Safety Council pages (see the COR guide for details), but the WorkSafeBC pages we read do not state a figure, so confirm it with your partner.

Records history matters too. BCCSA says there should be at least 6 months of supporting evidence, with 12 recommended, and the BC Forest Safety Council reviews the 12 months before an audit. Some partners publish no minimum, so ask. Whatever the number, start keeping records now.

The checklist: 13 elements and what to have ready

For each element, tick it when you could put the evidence in front of an auditor today. Citations are to the Workers Compensation Act (WCA) and the OHS Regulation (OHSR). A one-page printable version is free.

1. Health and safety policy

OHSR s.3.3(a)

  • A signed, dated policy statement with a review date. Our OHS policy statement template is free.
  • Proof workers have seen it: posted, covered in orientation, or acknowledged.

2. Hazard assessment and control

OHSR s.3.3(b)–(c), ss.3.9–3.10

  • A hazard register by task or area, with risk ratings and controls.
  • Field-level checks (JSA or daily FLHA) done before work and updated after changes.

3. Safe work practices

WCA s.21 · OHSR s.3.3

  • Written practices that match how the work is really done, such as a safe work procedure.
  • A record that workers were shown them.

4. Safe job procedures

OHSR Parts 9, 10 and 12 · s.3.3

  • Written procedures for each high-risk task, kept where the work happens: for example a confined space entry permit, lockout/tagout procedure or hot work permit.
  • Completed permits or procedure sign-offs showing they are actually used.

5. Company rules

WCA s.22

  • Written rules and a progressive response to breaches.
  • Signed worker acknowledgments.

6. Personal protective equipment

OHSR Part 8

  • A hazard-based PPE list by task.
  • Records of PPE training and issue.

7. Preventive maintenance

OHSR ss.4.3, 4.9–4.10

  • An equipment list with a maintenance schedule.
  • Signed inspection and maintenance entries.

8. Training and communication

OHSR ss.3.23–3.25 · WCA ss.37, 43–45

  • Orientation records for every new or reassigned worker.
  • Safety meeting minutes across the year. A toolbox talk record covers the short crew talks.

9. Workplace inspections

OHSR ss.3.5, 3.7–3.8

  • Completed inspection checklists on your set schedule.
  • A corrective-action log showing items closed out.

10. Investigations and reporting

WCA ss.68–72

  • Written investigations with causes and follow-up actions, for example on an incident investigation report.
  • A record of what you reported and when.

11. Emergency preparedness

OHSR Part 4

  • A written plan with contacts, roles and muster points. The emergency response plan template is free.
  • Drill or practice records.
  • First aid is set out separately in OHSR Part 3. The free first aid requirements calculator shows your minimum.

12. Statistics and records

OHSR s.3.3(f)

  • One place where the records can be found quickly.
  • A simple summary of incidents and corrective actions.

13. Legislation

WCA s.21(2)(f)

  • A posted notice saying where the Act and Regulation are kept.
  • A record that workers know where to find them.

What to do this week

  1. Mark each element in place, partly in place or missing. The free COR gap check does this in about two minutes and points to the document that closes each gap.
  2. Fix the lowest elements first. A single element under 50% can fail the audit, so a missing element matters more than a polished one.
  3. Start the records trail. Inspections, orientations, meetings and investigations need a history, and that time cannot be skipped later.
  4. Brief your crew. Make sure workers can say where the policy is posted, how the field-level check works and what the emergency plan is.
  5. Call your certifying partner to confirm the audit tool, the evidence they expect and the audit options for a business your size.

Documents are not certification. Templates and the kit give you the written program an audit looks for. Certification is awarded through your certifying partner after a passed audit.
