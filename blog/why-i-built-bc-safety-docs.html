Why I built BC Safety Docs.

November 27, 2026  ·  Derek Henderson  ·  2 min read

I work in HazMat and confined space rescue in BC, and I have for more than 15 years.

The job that started this was at a remote site. A welding crew, the client's crew and a safety crew were all on site and ready to go. The permit needed one signature, from a supervisor who was stuck in a meeting in Calgary. Nobody could start without it. We stood in the rain for about three hours, and a two-hour job took six to eight.

Nothing about the work was unusual. The problem was the paperwork: it wasn't organized, it wasn't ready, and everything depended on one person being reachable. I wished we had had everything set up ahead of time.

Building these documents has shown me how scattered the requirements are. They sit across the Workers Compensation Act, the OHS Regulation, WorkSafeBC guidelines and each certifying partner's audit tool, and the wording isn't always the same. Many templates I found were blank pages or US forms that don't match the BC Regulation.

So I wrote the documents I wished we'd had. They are specific to BC, short enough to use on a real job, and cited to the section they come from. I also check them. In July 2026 I audited my own confined space permit and found seven wrong citations. I fixed them and published the corrected list.

They're meant for employers with fewer than 20 workers who want to run their own safety program and prepare for a COR audit without finding out at the audit that a document is missing. The free templates and tools cover single jobs. The COR Small-Employer Kit puts the full written program together: 18 documents mapped to the 13 COR elements.

These are working tools, not legal advice and not certification. Your certifying partner awards COR after a passed audit. If you find something wrong or out of date, email info@bcsafetydocs.com and I'll check it.

Derek Henderson
