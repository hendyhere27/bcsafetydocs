The problem

Generic templates don't cut it on a BC worksite.

US-based forms miss BC classification

BC uses a three-tier atmospheric hazard classification — Low, Moderate, High — under s.9.1 and s.9.25. Most templates online are built for OSHA, not WorkSafeBC.

Free WorkSafeBC samples are starting points

WorkSafeBC's own samples don't have atmospheric reading columns, per-hazard standby person requirements, or amendment signature blocks needed for a real entry.

Enterprise software is overkill

SafetyCulture and SafetyEvolution charge $25–$30 per user per month. For a crew doing one confined space entry a week, that's $100+/month for a permit you can download free here.

Why “good” oxygen doesn't mean a safe atmosphere

A normal oxygen reading (19.5%–20.9%) can pass while H2S or CO is present at fatal concentrations. The math: going from 20.9% to the 19.5% floor represents about 14,000 ppm of total displacement. A lethal dose of H2S (IDLH ~100 ppm) or CO (fatal in hours at ~200 ppm) is well under 2% of that budget — small enough that it won't move your O2 reading outside the normal range at all. O2, LEL, H2S, and CO are four independent tests. A clean O2 number does not clear the other three.

Checked against the regulation

We audited our own templates. We found 7 citation errors and fixed them.

In July 2026 every Part 9 reference in our permit and tools was re-checked against the live WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation. Seven section references were wrong or overstated. All seven were corrected in the permit, on this site and in our free MCP server. The corrected references:

  1. 1Low-hazard standby person: now s.9.34 (was cited to the wrong section).
  2. 2Retest after everyone has left for more than 20 minutes: now s.9.25(3).
  3. 3Continuous monitoring where flammable gas could exceed 20% LEL: now s.9.25(6).
  4. 4Moderate-hazard ventilation: now s.9.30 (was cited to s.9.31).
  5. 5Supplied-air / SCBA determination: now s.9.28(a) (was cited to s.9.29, Inerting).
  6. 6No natural ventilation for high-hazard atmospheres: now s.9.33(2)(a).
  7. 7The 19.5% oxygen figure was presented as a Part 9 entry requirement. It comes from the s.1.1 definition of “oxygen deficient” (under 19.5%); Part 9 itself sets no numeric oxygen floor for entry, so we now describe it that way.

We also added a standing warning to the permit's atmospheric log and our tools: a normal oxygen reading clears the oxygen hazard only. H2S, CO and flammable gas are separate tests. Why a 20.9% reading doesn't mean safe →
