    How the penalty is set

    The rules behind the number.

    • Whether to penalize at all. WorkSafeBC considers a penalty when a violation is high risk, repeated, intentional, involves s.73 or s.79 or a stop order, or the circumstances otherwise warrant one. Many violations end with an order, not a penalty.
    • Basic amount. 0.5% of assessable payroll for the full calendar year before the violation, with a $1,250 minimum and a ceiling of half the statutory maximum.
    • Circumstances. Each aggravating circumstance doubles the basic amount, and the doublings add: one makes it 2×, two make it 4×, three make it 6×.
    • Repeats. Each prior penalty for the same or a similar violation within 3 years doubles it again (2×, 4×, 8×). With two or more, WorkSafeBC may double once more if earlier penalties didn't change behaviour.
    • Adjustments. In exceptional circumstances the amount can move up or down by up to 30%, and WorkSafeBC can add an estimate of any money saved by breaking the rule.
    • Discretionary penalties. For a high-risk violation committed intentionally or recklessly that kills or seriously and permanently injures a worker, WorkSafeBC can set any amount up to the maximum.
    • Ceiling. The statutory maximum is $816,148.69 for 2026, adjusted every year.
    • Due diligence. No penalty may be imposed if the employer shows it took all reasonable care to prevent the violation (s.95(3)).

    Sources: Workers Compensation Act ss.94–95; WorkSafeBC Prevention Manual items P2-95-1, P2-95-2, P2-95-5 and P2-95-9; WorkSafeBC OHS-related CPI adjustments. Checked 26 November 2026.

    Questions

    Before you rely on the number.

    Is this what WorkSafeBC will charge me?

    No. It applies the published formula to the figures you enter. WorkSafeBC first decides whether a penalty is warranted at all, then sets the amount, and can adjust it for exceptional circumstances or add any financial benefit from the violation. Treat the number as the scale of the risk, not a quote.

    What counts as high risk?

    WorkSafeBC names six: entering an excavation over 1.2 m (4 ft) contrary to the Regulation, working over 3 m (10 ft) without effective fall protection, entering a confined space without pre-entry testing and inspection, disturbing asbestos without necessary precautions, hand falling or bucking without necessary precautions, and working near combustible dust without necessary precautions. Other violations are judged on how likely an incident is and how serious the injury would be.

    What about OHS citations?

    A citation is a smaller, separate penalty for failing to comply with a WorkSafeBC order or a required compliance report. In 2026 the maximum is $1,312.96; a first citation is half that, and a repeat within 3 years is the full amount. This estimator covers the larger s.95 penalties only.

    Are penalties public?

    Yes. WorkSafeBC publishes penalized employers' names, the amount and a description of the violation in a searchable database on its website.

    Can I ask for a review?

    Yes. Employers can ask WorkSafeBC's Review Division to review a penalty decision. Check the deadline in your penalty letter; it is short.

    Do you store what I enter?

    No. The estimate is worked out in your browser and nothing you enter is sent to us.
