    How the incentive works

    The rules behind the number.

    • Formula. Assessable payroll × (classification unit base rate ÷ 100) × 10%, worked out for each classification unit in your COR.
    • Minimum. WorkSafeBC pays at least the lesser of $1,000 or 75% of the premiums you paid. For a small employer this often is the number.
    • Timing. You have to be certified first. WorkSafeBC starts considering payments in the second quarter of the year after certification and expects to credit your assessment account by the end of June.
    • Conditions. A valid certificate, an active account in good standing, and no exceptions such as late payroll reporting, an administrative penalty or a health and safety conviction.
    • Per-worker cap. Assessable earnings are capped per worker ($127,500 for 2026), so very high payrolls spread over few workers count for less.

    Source: WorkSafeBC — COR incentives, checked 9 October 2026.

    Questions

    Before you rely on the number.

    Is the incentive guaranteed?

    No. You have to pass a certification audit through your sector's certifying partner, keep the certificate valid with annual maintenance audits, and stay eligible. WorkSafeBC decides the amount. This is an estimate from the figures you enter and WorkSafeBC's published rules.

    Why does my result say $1,000?

    Because of the minimum. If 10% of your base premium is under $1,000, WorkSafeBC pays the lesser of $1,000 or 75% of the premiums you paid. A business with a premium of a few thousand dollars lands on that minimum.

    Base rate or net rate?

    WorkSafeBC's formula uses the classification unit base rate. Your net rate also includes experience-rating discounts or surcharges from your claims history, so it can differ. Use the base rate shown on your assessment rate notification.

    What does getting certified cost?

    This estimate leaves it out. Budget for your time, any registration or program fee from your certifying partner, an external auditor if you use one, and training for an internal auditor. Fees differ by partner and change over time, so ask yours.

    Do I need the COR kit to get certified?

    No. COR is awarded by your certifying partner after an audit, and many partners publish free templates. The kit is an editable, regulation-cited starting set of the written program documents; it's for people who would rather adapt than draft from scratch.

    What do you do with my email?

    We email you the breakdown. If you tick the box we'll also send a few short COR-readiness emails, and you can unsubscribe any time. We store your email, the figures you entered, and the result. See the privacy policy.
