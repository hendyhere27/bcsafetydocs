COR readiness checklist

BCSAFETYDOCS

Tick each element when you could put the evidence in front of an auditor today. Unticked boxes are your to-do list for audit prep.

1. Health & safety policyOHSR s.3.3(a) A signed, dated policy statement with a review date.Proof workers have seen it: posted, in orientation, or acknowledged.
2. Hazard assessment & controlOHSR s.3.3(b)–(c), ss.3.9–3.10 A hazard register by task or area, with risk ratings and controls.Field-level checks (JSA or daily FLHA) done before work, and updated after changes.
3. Safe work practicesWCA s.21 · OHSR s.3.3 Written practices that match how the work is really done.A record that workers were shown them.
4. Safe job proceduresOHSR Parts 9, 10, 12 · s.3.3 Written procedures for each high-risk task, kept where the work happens.Completed permits or procedure sign-offs showing they are actually used.
5. Company rulesWCA s.22 Written rules and a progressive response to breaches.Signed worker acknowledgments.
6. Personal protective equipmentOHSR Part 8 A hazard-based PPE list by task.Records of PPE training and issue.
7. Preventive maintenanceOHSR ss.4.3, 4.9–4.10 An equipment list with a maintenance schedule.Signed inspection and maintenance entries.
8. Training & communicationOHSR ss.3.23–3.25 · WCA ss.37, 43–45 Orientation records for every new or reassigned worker.Safety meeting minutes across the year.
9. Workplace inspectionsOHSR ss.3.5, 3.7–3.8 Completed inspection checklists on your set schedule.A corrective-action log showing items closed out.
10. Investigations & reportingWCA ss.68–72 Written investigations with causes and follow-up actions.A record of what you reported and when.
11. Emergency preparednessOHSR Part 4 A written plan with contacts, roles and muster points.Drill or practice records.
12. Statistics & recordsOHSR s.3.3(f) One place where the records can be found quickly.A simple summary of incidents and corrective actions.
13. LegislationWCA s.21(2)(f) A posted notice saying where the Act and Regulation are kept.A record that workers know where to find them.

Scoring: audits are scored two ways: 80% overall and at least 50% in every element (stated by BCCSA, AgSafe, go2HR, Energy Safety Canada and the BC Forest Safety Council). One weak element can fail an audit that is strong overall.

Records: auditors want to see the system actually running. BCCSA says at least 6 months of supporting evidence, with 12 recommended; the BC Forest Safety Council reviews the 12 months before an audit. Some partners publish no minimum, so ask yours.

This is a self-check against the national 13-element COR framework, not an audit score or a pass/fail prediction. Certifying partners use their own audit tools (Energy Safety Canada, for example, uses 10 elements), so check yours. Free gap check with matching documents: bcsafetydocs.com/cor-gap-check.
