      How it works

      The rules behind the answer.

      • Workplace class. Remote means an ambulance can't drive from its base in 30 minutes or less. Less-accessible means a work area ambulance personnel can't safely reach, or a ferry. Neither is class 1, remote only is class 2, less-accessible only is class 3, both is class 4.
      • The table. Your class picks Table 3-1, 3-2, 3-3 or 3-4. Workers present and your hazard rating pick the row and column.
      • Every shift. WorkSafeBC says all attendants the table requires must be at the workplace during every shift where they are required, not spread across shifts.
      • "(transport)" attendants. That endorsement is about the attendant's training. It does not by itself mean you need an emergency transportation vehicle; the table lists that separately.
      • The written assessment. The table is the minimum. Section 3.16 also requires a written assessment of your workers, locations, hazards, likely injuries, barriers to first aid and transport times, prepared with your joint committee or worker representative, reviewed every 12 months and after significant changes. Anything it shows you need is required too.
      • Old certificates. Level 1, 2 and 3 certificates issued before November 1, 2024 count as basic, intermediate and advanced until they expire. That transition rule ends November 1, 2027.

      Source: OHS Regulation ss.3.14–3.21 and Schedule 3-A (B.C. Reg. 132/2023), BC Laws consolidation current to November 3, 2026; WorkSafeBC first aid FAQ. Checked 26 November 2026. WorkSafeBC also has its own online first aid assessment.

      Questions

      Before you rely on the answer.

      Is the table everything I need?

      No, it's the floor. Section 3.16(1)(b) requires whatever else your written assessment shows is needed to get injured workers first aid and transport promptly: more attendants for spread-out crews, extra kits, a vehicle on site, or air transport arrangements.

      What if workers from several employers share the site?

      Section 3.20 makes the prime contractor responsible for assessing first aid for all the workers on site and doing what is reasonably practicable to provide it.

      When does a first aid room mean overnight care?

      If the table requires a first aid room and the trip to a hospital with an emergency department would take more than 2 hours under normal conditions, Schedule 3-A section 5 also requires an attendant available for overnight care and a room equipped for at least 2 injured workers.

      What if air is our only way out?

      Section 3.17.1 applies: before work starts you need arrangements with an air service for an appropriate aircraft, a stretcher that fits it, a daily availability check, and communication between the pilot and the attendant.

      What about a worker alone?

      Schedule 3-A sets no minimum for a worker working alone, but you still have to be able to get them first aid and medical treatment promptly (s.3.16(1)(b)). Working alone also has its own check-in rules in Part 4.
